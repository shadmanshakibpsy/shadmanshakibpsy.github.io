01 About

A researcher at the meeting point of perception, action, and technology.

I study a deceptively simple question: how do we perceive what we are able to do?

As a graduate student in the Experimental Psychology PhD program at the University of Southern Mississippi, I work with Dr. Alen Hajnal on perception and action. My focus is affordances — the actions an environment makes possible, such as stepping over an obstacle or ducking under a barrier — and how factors like body size, posture, head movements and illumination shape the way observers perceive them.

To test these questions with precision, I build virtual-reality and real-life experiments in virtual reality. I designed and deployed two fully functional Unity experimental tasks using C# scripts and game logic, then validated them to produce reliable, reproducible behavioral data. Alongside research, I have served as a teaching assistant across Eleven psychology courses, from General Psychology to Sensation and Perception.

02 Research

Research interests

My work sits within the ecological tradition of perception — studying how observers pick up information directly from their environment to guide action.

/ 01

Affordance Perception

How observers perceive the actions an environment makes available, whether a gap can be crossed or a barrier passed.

/ 02

Perception–Action Coupling

The tight loop between what we perceive and what we do, and how it continuously guides movement and behavior.

/ 03

Body-Scaled Perception

How the dimensions of one's own body calibrate judgments of what is reachable, passable, or possible.

/ 04

Ecological Psychology

A Gibsonian approach: studying perception directly within the organism–environment system rather than in isolation.

/ 05

Virtual-Reality Methods

Designing immersive, fully controllable environments to measure perception and action with experimental precision.

/ 06

Exploration & Illumination

How active looking, posture, and lighting conditions influence what observers perceive they are able to do.

Featured · Master's Thesis

How do perceptual exploration and body size influence the affordances of stepping over and ducking under obstacles?

My thesis investigates how an observer's body dimensions and active perceptual exploration shape judgments of whether obstacles can be stepped over or ducked under — examined through controlled, immersive virtual-reality tasks.
